# Recurring Terms in this Course
- **State Estimation:** The mathematical and algorithmic process of inferring unknown quantities (the system's "state") from uncertain, noisy sensor measurements and control inputs. In robotics, this involves tracking not just the robot's current pose (position and orientation) and velocity, but often the positions of surrounding environment features or dynamic obstacles. ^State-Estimation
- **Localization:** The specific problem of determining a robot's pose relative to an established external coordinate frame or a known map. By continuously comparing live sensor observations (such as LiDAR scans, camera feeds or wheel odometry) against recognizable reference landmarks or a pre-built map, the robot resolves ambiguities about its exact placement in the environment. ^localization
- **Mapping:** The process of constructing a spatial model of the environment using onboard sensors such as cameras, ultrasonic sensors, and LiDAR. A map captures navigable pathways, boundaries, and static or dynamic obstacles, providing the spatial representation needed for a robot to reason about traversable spaces and understand its surroundings. ^mapping
- **SLAM (Simultaneous Localization and Mapping):** A fundamental robotics paradigm where a mobile robot builds a consistent map of an unknown environment while concurrently keeping track of its own location within that evolving map. Because localization requires a map and mapping requires an accurate location, SLAM solves this "chicken-and-egg" problem simultaneously through probabilistic estimation techniques <u>Kalman filters</u> or graph-based optimization. ^SLAM
- **Navigation:** The overarching autonomous capability that enables a robot to determine its current location, decide where to go, and physically travel from a starting position (Point A) to a target destination (Point B) safely and efficiently. It integrates localization, mapping, path planning and obstacle avoidance into a cohesive closed-loop system. ^navigation
- **Motion Planning:** The computational process of determining a feasible sequence of intermediate states, configurations or trajectories to transition the robot from a start state to a goal state. It accounts for geometric constraints (avoiding collisions with obstacles), kinematic constraints (how the robot can turn or steer), and dynamic limits (velocity, acceleration and torque constraints). ^motion-planning
- **Robot Control:** The execution layer responsible for generating and sending specific actuator commands (such as motor voltages, wheel torques, or steering angles) to ensure the physical robot actually follows the intended trajectory. By utilizing feedback mechanisms (like PID or Model Predictive Control), it corrects for real-world disturbances, slip, and drift to keep the robot on course. ^robot-control

# Probability
- **Random Variable:** A **random variable** is a mathematical rule that assigns a concrete numerical value to each possible outcome of an uncertain event - such as mapping the physical results of two coin flips $(HH, HT, TH, TT)$ to numbers $(2,1,1,0)$ representing the count of heads. It translates descriptive real-world outcomes into numbers you can calculate with, and these output values can be any real numbers (they do not need to add up to 1). ^random-variable
- **Probability Mass Function (PMF):** A **probability mass function (PMF)** is the function that assigns an exact probability to each specific numerical value that a discrete random variable can take, written as $P(X=x)$. It gives the probability distribution for the random variable's outputs, adhering to two strict rules: each individual probability must lie between 0 and 1 $(0 <= P(X=x) <=1)$, and the sum of the probabilities across all possible outcomes must always total exactly 1 ($\sum P(X = x) = 1$). ^probability-mass-function
